# Concept prompts and reference edits

Use only the fields that clarify the current request. A comparison board is a selection aid, not a production asset.

## New direction

```text
Use case: logo-brand
Brand: <exact name and case>
Product: <audience, job, place or subject that gives the mark meaning>
Concept: <one specific visual idea, distinct from the other directions>
Shape: <silhouette and a small number of essential components>
Typography: <wordmark characteristics or approved font>
Palette: <approved roles, or explicit freedom to explore>
Presentation: same geometry on light and dark surfaces, large lockup and symbol-only study
Avoid: <specific mismatches: e.g. detailed scenery, forced initials, mascot features>
```

Do not pack several unrelated alternatives into one tiny image. Keep optional labels outside the mark. For initial brand exploration, the number and style of concepts should follow the user, not a mandatory mascot or monogram recipe.

## Color-only edit

Inspect the supplied image before editing. Use the image tool's actual reference mechanism.

```text
Edit this reference. Change colors only.
Preserve the symbol geometry, component proportions, wordmark letterforms,
negative-space gaps, scale, and layout.
Light: <background, symbol roles, wordmark>
Dark: <background, symbol roles, wordmark>
Keep the same symbol in both appearances.
```

Inspect whether the tool actually preserved the shape. Correct a drift that changes the selected identity. Image generation can help with selection and controlled variations; use a clean vector source for final small icons.

## Review criteria

- Is the outline recognizable before seeing the wordmark?
- Does the idea fit the whole product, rather than one incidental category?
- Do light/dark comparisons preserve geometry?
- Does the result resemble a familiar major brand enough to weaken the direction?
- Can the design be reproduced in simple vector paths and a single-color mask?
- Does the cleaned mark retain its gaps under realistic small-size and mask previews?

Acknowledge a weak result rather than praising every generation. These criteria support judgment; they do not establish legal uniqueness or predict user preference.
